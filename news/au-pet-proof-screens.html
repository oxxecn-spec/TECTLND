Heartworm — A Deadly Mosquito-Borne Disease

Heartworm (Dirofilaria immitis) is a parasitic worm transmitted by mosquitoes that can be fatal to dogs, cats, and other animals . The disease is endemic to Australia, with the highest prevalence along the eastern seaboard — from northern Queensland down to Victoria .

Key facts about heartworm in Australia:

  • Transmission: When a mosquito bites an infected animal, it picks up microscopic heartworm larvae. The larvae mature in the mosquito over 10-14 days and are then transmitted to the next animal the mosquito bites .
  • Lifecycle: Once injected, the larvae travel to the heart and lungs, where they mature into adult worms up to 30 cm long .
  • Health impact: Adult worms can block blood flow in the lungs and heart, leading to heart failure and death .
  • Prevention: While there is preventive medication, the best defence is preventing mosquito bites .

Australia has 6 mosquito species known to transmit heartworm. The most important vector is Culex annulirostris — the same species that also transmits Japanese encephalitis virus and Ross River virus .

🩺 Heartworm in Australian Pets: Heartworm is endemic to Australia, with the highest prevalence along the eastern seaboard from Queensland to Victoria. The disease is transmitted by mosquitoes and can be fatal. Prevention relies on avoiding mosquito bites .

Ross River Virus — A Threat to Pets and Their Owners

Ross River virus (RRV) is the most common mosquito-borne disease in Australia, with approximately 5,000 cases annually . While RRV is best known for affecting humans, it also poses a risk to animals. Australia's wildlife — including kangaroos, wallabies, and possums — serve as reservoir hosts for the virus .

Mosquitoes that feed on infected wildlife can transmit RRV to humans and domestic animals. Dogs and horses can also be infected with RRV, though they are typically not the primary reservoir .

With Australian case numbers surging — RRV cases nearly doubled from 1,710 in 2023 to 3,375 in 2024 — the risk to pets and their owners is greater than ever .

🦘 RRV and Animals: Kangaroos, wallabies, and possums are reservoir hosts for Ross River virus. Mosquitoes that feed on infected wildlife can transmit the virus to dogs, horses, and humans. RRV cases in Australia nearly doubled in 2024 .

The Problem with Standard Screens in Australian Pet Homes

Australian pet owners know the frustration all too well. Standard screens are no match for:

  • Dogs excited to get outside — they charge through flimsy screens, leaving gaping holes
  • Cats scratching at windows — they use screens as scratching posts, shredding the mesh
  • Puppies and kittens — they chew on everything, including screens
  • Repeated daily use — pets going in and out of doors wears out the mesh

One Australian customer review notes: "I have two large dogs and they destroyed the previous screen within a week. I needed something that could handle them" . Another review states: "My cat kept climbing the screen and tearing holes in it. I was replacing screens every few months" .

The cost adds up. A professional screen replacement in Australia typically costs $200-$500 per screen . And in areas with high mosquito pressure — like Queensland, northern NSW, and the Torres Strait — these damaged screens are more than just an expense. They're a health risk.

Other Mosquito-Borne Diseases Affecting Australian Pets

Beyond heartworm and RRV, Australian pets face other mosquito-borne threats:

  • Barmah Forest virus — similar to RRV, transmitted by mosquitoes in coastal and inland regions
  • Japanese encephalitis virus — has been detected across multiple Australian states and is transmitted by Culex annulirostris .
  • Dengue and Zika viruses — while not endemic in Australia, the establishment of Aedes albopictus (Asian tiger mosquito) on the mainland raises the risk of future outbreaks affecting both humans and animals .

With Australia's national case numbers for mosquito-borne viruses nearly doubling between 2023 and 2024 , the importance of protecting pets from mosquito bites has never been greater .
