Kent – The UK's Gateway for Invasive Mosquitoes

Kent's geographic position makes it the most likely entry point for invasive mosquito species into the UK. The county has:

  • Busiest ports — Dover and Folkestone handle millions of passengers and vehicles annually from continental Europe
  • Eurotunnel and Eurostar terminals — Direct rail connections to France, with thousands of passengers and cargo shipments daily
  • M20 motorway — A major transport corridor linking the ports to London and the rest of the UK, with motorway service stations where mosquitoes can be carried by vehicles
  • Proximity to France — The Asian tiger mosquito is already established in northern France, including Calais and the Normandy coast

UKHSA has identified Kent as a high-priority surveillance area, with enhanced monitoring at ports, motorway service stations, and other high-risk locations .

Asian Tiger Mosquito – The Aggressive Invader

The Asian tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus) is one of the most invasive and dangerous mosquito species in the world. Native to Southeast Asia, it has spread to every continent except Antarctica .

Why It's a Threat

  • Aggressive daytime biter — Unlike most mosquitoes that bite at dawn and dusk, the Asian tiger mosquito bites all day long .
  • Disease vector — It can transmit dengue, Zika, chikungunya, and West Nile virus .
  • Breeds in small containers — It breeds in birdbaths, tyres, plant saucers, bottle caps, and children's toys — making urban and suburban environments ideal habitat .
  • Hardy and adaptable — It can survive in temperate climates and lay drought-resistant eggs that can survive for months, hatching when conditions are favourable .
  • Readily transported — It travels easily in used tyres, bamboo, and other cargo .

The Distinguishing Mark

The Asian tiger mosquito is named for its black body with distinctive white stripes — including a single white stripe down the centre of its head and back .

🦟 Asian Tiger Mosquito Fact: The Asian tiger mosquito is an aggressive daytime biter that can transmit dengue, Zika, chikungunya, and West Nile virus. It breeds in small containers and is readily transported in tyres and cargo .

Kent Detections – What We Know

Kent has been the site of multiple detections of Asian tiger mosquitoes in recent years:

  • August 2024: Aedes albopictus was detected near a fast-food drive-through at a service station on the M20 motorway in Kent . This is the first detection since 2019 .
  • 2016-2019: Aedes albopictus eggs were detected at a truck stop in Kent, with enhanced surveillance preventing the species from establishing .
  • 2017: An adult male Aedes aegypti was discovered near Liverpool — the only previous confirmed finding of that species in the UK .

The M20 detection is particularly concerning because motorway service stations are high-risk locations for invasive mosquito introduction — mosquitoes can be carried by vehicles, and the service station environment provides both water sources (drainage, puddles) and human hosts for blood feeding .

UKHSA has now established enhanced surveillance at 117 locations across England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, including seaports, airports, and roadside locations .

Climate Change – Kent's Warming Climate

Climate change is making Kent warmer and more humid, creating conditions that are increasingly favourable for Asian tiger mosquito establishment .

The Asian tiger mosquito is already established in northern France, including the areas around Calais and the Normandy coast . With the Channel Tunnel and frequent ferry crossings, the journey from established populations in France to potential new populations in Kent is remarkably short .

UKHSA modelling shows that the risk of Aedes albopictus establishment in the UK increases significantly with rising temperatures . Milder winters, warmer summers, and increased humidity are all contributing factors .

As Dr. Jolyon Medlock, head of the Medical Entomology team at UKHSA, has noted: "The UK is no longer free from the risk of invasive mosquitoes. Climate change and increased international travel mean that the conditions for these species to survive and establish in the UK are becoming more favourable" .

West Nile Virus – Culex Mosquitoes in Kent

Beyond the Asian tiger mosquito, Kent also faces risks from Culex mosquitoes that carry West Nile virus. A 2023 UKHSA surveillance study collected 2,157 adult Culex mosquitoes from 200 sites across England and Wales — including Kent .

Of particular concern is the presence of Culex modestus, a known West Nile virus vector that was last recorded in the UK in 1937 but reappeared in 2010 and is now established in Essex and Kent .

In 2025, the BMJ reported that West Nile virus fragments were detected in UK mosquitoes for the first time . This serves as a wake-up call for Kent residents.
