NSW's 2024-2025 Arbovirus Season — The Numbers

The NSW Health 2024-2025 Arbovirus Surveillance Report reveals a significant disease burden. Ross River virus remained the predominant mosquito-borne disease reported in NSW, with notifications exceeding the 10-year average [citation:2].

Key statistics:

  • 498 RRV notifications (45 confirmed, 453 probable) [citation:2]
  • 90 BFV notifications (4 confirmed, 86 probable) [citation:2]
  • 5 JEV cases acquired in NSW [citation:2]
  • 1 locally acquired case of Kunjin virus [citation:2]

Japanese Encephalitis Virus — A Deadly New Threat

In 2022, the unprecedented and widespread emergence of Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) across multiple Australian states prompted the declaration of a Communicable Disease Incident of National Significance. Since then, JEV has become established in NSW.

In the 2025-2026 season, JEV detections have been earlier than expected. In November 2025, JEV was detected in mosquitoes in Victoria, and in early January 2026, Japanese encephalitis virus was detected in a sentinel chicken in the Upper Lachlan Shire near Goulburn [citation:1]. This indicates that JEV is likely circulating in the area [citation:1].

A probable case of JE is being investigated in a man in his 60s who was likely exposed to the virus in the Riverina region in late December 2025 [citation:1].

JEV is potentially fatal — at least 15 JEV fatalities have been recorded in Australia, including two in NSW earlier in 2025 [citation:8]. In February and March 2025, NSW Health reported the deaths of two men aged in their 70s from JE infections [citation:8].

🧬 JEV in NSW: 15+ fatalities nationally, including two in NSW. 2026 detections: sentinel chicken in Goulburn (Jan 2026); probable human case in Riverina (Dec 2025). 60 NSW LGAs now considered at elevated risk [citation:1][citation:3].

Kunjin Virus — A West Nile Relative

Kunjin virus is a subtype of West Nile virus that can cause severe neurological illness [citation:3]. In November 2025, Kunjin virus was detected in a sentinel chicken blood sample in Western NSW at Cowra [citation:4].

NSW Health's Director of Health Protection, Dr Stephen Conaty, said these early season detections are "concerning," with mosquito numbers likely to increase with warmer weather [citation:3].

There is no specific treatment or vaccine for Kunjin virus. The best way to avoid infection is to avoid being bitten by mosquitoes [citation:4].

🧬 Kunjin Virus in NSW: Detected in sentinel chicken at Cowra, Western NSW, in November 2025. A subtype of West Nile virus that can cause severe neurological illness. No vaccine or specific treatment available [citation:4][citation:14].

Ross River Virus — The Predominant Disease

Ross River virus is the most common mosquito-borne disease in NSW and Australia [citation:2]. It can cause a rash, fever and joint pain (arthralgia). While most people recover within weeks, in some cases symptoms can persist, hindering quality of life [citation:12].

In the 2024-2025 season, RRV notifications were higher than the 10-year average [citation:2]. Cases were highest in inland regions — particularly Hunter New England, Murrumbidgee, and Western NSW — with elevated activity also reported in coastal areas including Northern NSW and the Mid North Coast [citation:2].

RRV can be spread by more than 40 different mosquito species, making it difficult to control through vector management alone [citation:12].

NSW Health Protection Measures

NSW Health's surveillance program monitors mosquito-borne viruses through:

  • Sentinel chicken flocks — provide early warning of virus circulation [citation:5]
  • Mosquito trapping — monitors abundance and virus presence [citation:5]
  • Human notification reporting — tracks disease cases [citation:2]

NSW Health urges residents to [citation:4][citation:6]:

  • Cover windows and doors with insect screens and check for gaps
  • Apply repellent containing DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus
  • Wear long sleeves and pants, especially at dawn and dusk
  • Eliminate standing water — remove items that may collect water such as old tyres and empty pots

For JEV, a safe, effective, and free vaccine is available to anyone who lives or routinely works in 60 NSW Local Government Areas west of the dividing range and in northern NSW [citation:3].

🛡️ NSW Health Advice: "Cover windows and doors with insect screens and check there are no gaps." — NSW Health [citation:4]. For JEV, a free vaccine is available for residents of 60 high-risk LGAs. For RRV, BFV and Kunjin — protection relies wholly on avoiding mosquito bites [citation:2][citation:3].
