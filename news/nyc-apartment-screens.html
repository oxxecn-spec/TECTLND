The NYC Renter's Dilemma – Screens Without Damage

If you rent in New York City, you know the rules. Most leases explicitly prohibit drilling into walls, window frames, or building structures. Security deposits can be forfeited for even small modifications. Yet without screens, NYC apartments face:

  • Mosquitoes – yes, even in NYC. West Nile virus is present in all five boroughs.
  • Flies and other insects – especially in ground-floor and garden apartments.
  • City dust and pollen – NYC's air quality can be challenging, especially in summer.
  • Pigeons and birds – can enter through unscreened windows in some buildings.

The H01 solves this problem with its industrial-strength double-sided tape installation. The tape bonds securely to window frames – aluminium, PVC, wood, metal, or glass – without any drilling, screws, or permanent modifications. And when you move out? The screen can be removed cleanly without leaving residue.

📋 NYC Co-op & Condo Approval: Many NYC co-op and condo boards require board approval for exterior modifications. The H01 is installed on the interior window frame and is completely invisible from the outside when retracted – often eliminating the need for board approval.

NYC Mosquito Threat – Yes, It's Real

Many New Yorkers mistakenly believe mosquitoes aren't a problem in the city. Wrong. The NYC Department of Health monitors mosquito activity from April through October, and West Nile virus is consistently detected in mosquitoes throughout the city. According to the CDC:

  • New York has reported hundreds of West Nile virus cases since the virus first arrived in 1999 – the very first outbreak in the US.
  • In 2024, New York City reported locally acquired dengue cases – a sign that mosquito-borne diseases are expanding into new regions.
  • The Asian tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus), which can transmit Zika, dengue, and chikungunya, is well-established in NYC.
  • Climate change is lengthening NYC's mosquito season, with more days suitable for disease transmission.

Worldwide, the threat is massive. According to the World Health Organization:

  • Over 700,000 people die from mosquito-borne diseases annually.
  • Malaria kills over 600,000 people each year, most of them children under five.
  • Dengue has increased 30-fold over the last 50 years, with over 5 million cases in 2023.
  • Zika remains a threat, with ongoing transmission in the Americas.
  • Chikungunya has spread to more than 110 countries.

In the first half of 2025 alone, global surveillance recorded over 2 million suspected dengue cases with more than 1,000 deaths, and over 8,500 Zika cases across the Americas and Africa. The mosquito threat is not going away – and it's reaching urban centers like NYC.

🧬 NYC Mosquito Facts: Over 40 mosquito species have been identified in New York City. The NYC Department of Health conducts active surveillance using traps and testing for West Nile virus. In recent years, the Aedes albopictus (Asian tiger mosquito) – a daytime biter and disease vector – has become increasingly common in the city. The H01's fine 1.2mm mesh blocks all of them.
