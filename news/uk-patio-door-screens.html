The UK Door Screen Challenge

UK homes feature a variety of door styles, each presenting unique challenges for insect screens:

  • French doors — Popular in period homes (Victorian, Edwardian, Georgian) and modern extensions. Two doors open outward or inward. Traditional screens are difficult to install without blocking both doors.
  • Patio doors (sliding) — Common in modern homes and extensions. The sliding mechanism requires screens that don't interfere with operation.
  • Bi-fold doors — Increasingly popular for garden rooms and rear extensions. Multiple panels fold to one side, making traditional screens impossible.
  • Stable doors — Traditional in rural and equestrian properties. Half-door design requires custom screen solutions.

Traditional door screens fail in one or more of these key areas:

  • Floor tracks — Collect dirt, create tripping hazards, and require regular cleaning
  • Bulky frames — Obstruct views and compromise the aesthetic of the doors
  • Fixed screens — Block access and make it impossible to fully open doors
  • No insect protection — Many UK homes simply go without screens, leaving doors open to mosquitoes and flies

  • No interference with bi-fold doors — The zipper track mounts on the frame, not the floor

Bi-Parting French Doors

Many UK French doors are bi-parting — both doors open outwards. The H01 can be installed with a removable centre guide that sits between the doors when closed, and recedes when opened — allowing full operation without interference.

UK Building Regulations — Part F and Doors

Under Approved Document F of the UK Building Regulations, door openings require background ventilation . In many UK homes, this is provided by trickle vents or door glazing. The H01's 1.2mm mesh with 60%+ open area complies with Part F guidance that screens must not reduce ventilator free area by more than 20% . This makes the H01 suitable for properties required to meet building regulations.

Bi-Fold Doors — The Ultimate Test

Bi-fold doors are increasingly popular in UK extensions and new homes. They present the ultimate test for insect screens:

  • Multiple panels — Each panel folds to one side
  • No central frame — When fully open, there's no vertical structure for a screen to mount to
  • Large openings — Standard bi-fold doors can be 3-6 meters wide

Standard solutions fail on bi-fold doors because they:

  • Require a floor track — Creates a tripping hazard across the full width
  • Block the view — Bulky frames obstruct the glass
  • Prevent full opening — Fixed screens block the fold

The H01's top-hung zipper track design works with bi-fold doors because:

  • No floor track — Safe and clean
  • Narrow cassette — Sits discreetly at the top of the door frame
  • Zipper guides — Mount on the side frames, not the folding panels
  • Invisible when retracted — Preserves the view
  • Compatible with sill heights — Can be installed even with low thresholds
